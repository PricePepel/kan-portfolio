# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

Three real audiences read this page, in roughly this order of stakes:

1. **AI lab and research recruiters** (Anthropic, OpenAI, DeepMind, Cohere, Mistral, university research groups). They will open DevTools and check the engineering. They are looking for: technical depth, system-design taste, evidence the person ships, not just talks. With Minerva admission confirmed, internship and research-track opportunities during the four-year program are the highest-leverage audience.
2. **Future Minerva collaborators, classmates, and faculty** searching for context on a peer or candidate. They want to know who this person is before working with them — projects, interests, working style.
3. **Curious technical visitors** from referrals, GitHub, X/Twitter, HackerNews. They are looking for: what this person actually builds, whether it's interesting.

The page must read identically credible to all three. No commercial intent visible to any of them.

## Product Purpose

Personal portfolio for Kan — 17 years old, Tashkent, Uzbekistan. Finishing 11th grade in June 2026, starts Minerva University (class of 2030) in August 2026. Four-year program rotating across six cities: San Francisco (year 1), then Seoul, Hyderabad, Berlin, Buenos Aires, London or Taipei.

The site exists to make Kan legible as a builder: someone who shipped production AI products at high-school age, led a 900-student academic council, taught web development to 80+ students at ITSTEP Academy, co-authored AI/FinTech research, and was Lead Software Developer at Saman AI Academy before starting university.

Success criteria: a reader who has never heard of Kan comes away with a clear, specific, non-generic impression of who this person is and what they build. Not "talented teenager", but specific: "the person who shipped a B2B SaaS AI hiring platform with idempotent UTC streak keys and JWKS verification at 17, on his way to Minerva."

The page does not sell anything. It documents work.

## Brand Personality

Technical, considered, warm.

The engineering rigor is real, but it manifests as craft and quiet confidence, not coldness or cleverness. The site should feel like reading the personal site of someone who would rather show you the work than tell you about themselves, except they are also clear about who they are.

A reader should leave thinking "this person takes their work seriously and is interesting", not "this person is selling something."

## References

- **fromanother.love**: dynamic visual flow, asymmetric balance, numbered sections, photography-forward. Studios show capability through the design itself.
- **teatrlalka.pl**: warmth, color as personality, pictographic elements that feel human.
- **thatfestivalsite.com**: generous whitespace, typography-driven hierarchy, large hero moments.
- **fjord.dev / robinrendle.com / matthiasott.com**: personal sites of real builders, no commerce, make the person legible through artifacts.

What specifically resonates: **typographic confidence, generous whitespace, dynamic motion that earns its place, warmth without preciousness.**

## Anti-references

- **Freelance portfolios with pricing tiers and "book a call" CTAs**: the previous incarnation of this same site. Wrong audience signal for the current goal. Anything that reads as transactional belongs elsewhere.
- **kawka.me**: ultra-restrained minimalism, grayscale, deliberately un-trendy. Too safe.
- **gov.uk**: institutional austerity, no personality, no craft.
- **The "available for hire" stripe** at the top of every developer personal site in 2024.

The pattern: anything that signals "for sale" or "boring on purpose."

## Design Principles

1. **Craft is the proof.** The site's execution is the strongest evidence the work is real.
2. **Warm technical.** Engineering rigor expressed as paper-and-ink restraint, not the default "AI dark mode" of #0a0a0a + neon.
3. **Commit to choices.** Bold palette, decisive typography, confident motion. No hedging.
4. **Motion as voice, not decoration.** Animation communicates attention to detail; never four animated layers competing for the same focal area.
5. **Show, do not sell.** Every section answers "what did you build / what are you working on", never "what can you do for me."
6. **The visitor is busy.** 60 seconds is the real budget. Make the first screen and the case tiles do most of the work.

## Audience-specific cues to include

- Age and stage are visible but not a sob story. The reader should know within 30 seconds that this is a high-school senior, not a 25-year-old freelancer.
- The trajectory is visible: Minerva University, class of 2030, starting August 2026, computer science track, rotating cities first year in San Francisco.
- Real artifacts dominate: four shipped projects, 900-student council, 80+ students taught, research co-authorship.
- No pricing. No "book a call." No "available." Mailto for contact is fine; that is how humans email humans.
